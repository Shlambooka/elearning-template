Positive feedback: amplifying change

Positive feedback is a loop where the system responds to a change by pushing in the same direction. The result is growth, acceleration, or — sometimes — runaway change.

Everyday example: a microphone and speaker

A small sound enters the mic, which is amplified through the speaker, which is picked up by the mic again — bigger this time — and round it goes. The familiar squeal is positive feedback in action.

Another: population growth

More people → more births → even more people. The change feeds itself.

Bringing it together

Both loops are about the direction of the response. Negative opposes change (stabilises). Positive reinforces change (amplifies). Neither is inherently "good" or "bad" — the label describes the mechanism, not the outcome.
